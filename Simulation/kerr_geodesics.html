How the orbits are computed

For constants E, L, Q and κ = −1 (massive) or 0 (light), Theorem 3.3 of the report gives (Σṙ)² = R(r) and (Σθ̇)² = Θ(θ), with

R(r) = Δ[κr² − (L − aE)² − Q] + [E(r² + a²) − aL]², Θ(θ) = Q + cos²θ [(κ + E²)a² − L²/sin²θ].

Changing the parameter to Mino time, dσ = dλ / Σ, decouples the two equations. The page integrates their derivatives d²r/dσ² = R′(r)/2 and d²θ/dσ² = Θ′(θ)/2, which pass smoothly through turning points, together with dφ/dσ and dt/dσ from Theorem 3.4 and dλ/dσ = Σ. The integrator is an adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4) scheme with relative tolerance 10⁻¹⁰. The drift readout reports the largest violation of (dr/dσ)² = R and (dθ/dσ)² = Θ along the orbit.

For bound orbits the page writes L = x√C and Q = (1 − x²)C with x = cos ι, and solves R(rp) = R(ra) = 0 for E and C by Newton's method, starting from a wide orbit and shrinking it step by step. Positions are drawn at x = √(r² + a²) sin θ cos φ, y = √(r² + a²) sin θ sin φ, z = r cos θ. A run stops when the particle reaches r+ + 0.05 (Boyer–Lindquist t diverges at the horizon), escapes to large r, or reaches the chosen duration.
